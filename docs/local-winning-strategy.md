base# Local Winning Strategy

NASA Space Apps 2026 · Cottbus · researched Saturday 14 November 2026, 13:30 CET

What NASA and local judges expect, and how to spend the hours we have to finish in the top of the Cottbus event and be nominated for global judging.

**The core fact:** judges at every level, Cottbus included, score five criteria from 1 to 5: Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation. Everything below is built on that rubric.

> **How to read this.** Part 1 is verified from official sources. Part 2 is our own strategy and is labelled as such. Cottbus is a first-time event, so there are no previous local winners to study. The closest evidence is last year's Global Winners, who all started as local nominees (see `space-apps-winner-study.md`).

## Part 1. Verified from official sources

### NASA's stated purpose

The Judging & Awards Guide says Space Apps is "at its core a collaborative event, not a competitive one". Every team that submits by the deadline gets a certificate.

### The judging criteria

The same five criteria apply "at every level of judging, from Local and Universal Events to Executive Judging". Each is scored 1 to 5. No weights are published.

| Criterion    | What the guide says it rewards                                                                                           | What a score of 5 looks like                                             |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Impact       | Addressing a problem, minor or major, and how many people it helps or inspires                                           | A significant problem, extensive reach, quality far above expectations   |
| Creativity   | Originality: a novel concept or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas                                               | A groundbreaking approach that challenges conventional methods           |
| Validity     | Grounded in science, technically feasible, complete, usable                                                              | Scientifically sound, fully developed, ready for real use, user-friendly |
| Relevance    | "Fully and directly addresses the challenge" and uses NASA data "as a core component rather than a superficial addition" | Deeply connected to the challenge goals, NASA data integral to success   |
| Presentation | Clear communication of the challenge, the solution and why it matters. Judged from the demo and the full project         | A structured, compelling, polished story                                 |

The guide adds that many teams excel at the first four and should "pay equal attention to the final criterion: presentation".

### How local winners are selected

- All submissions from a Local Event are evaluated by a panel of Local Judges.
- Local Judges are recruited, trained and organized by the Local Lead.
- They use the NASA criteria to select projects that advance. These are the Global Nominees.
- Local awards are optional and separate. Only officially submitted Global Nominees enter global judging.
- In 2025, about 1,290 of more than 11,500 projects were nominated. That is roughly one in nine.

### After the local stage

| Phase | Who judges                            | Outcome                                 | Date             |
| ----- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| 1     | Local Judges                          | Global Nominees                         | 3 December 2026  |
| 2     | NASA and partner-agency experts       | Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions | 17 December 2026 |
| 3     | Executive committee of agency leaders | 10 Global Winners, one per award        | 26 January 2027  |

### Eligibility for global judging

A project is only eligible if all of these hold:

- It responds to one official Space Apps challenge.
- Every member is registered for Cottbus, is not waitlisted and appears on the team's Members tab. No members can be added after submission closes.
- The team has at most six members.
- All required fields on the project page are complete.
- The demo is a video of 30 seconds or less, or a deck of at most seven slides including title and intro.
- The project page and all linked material are in English. A video may be spoken in another language if it has English subtitles.
- Every external resource (code, text, images) is listed in the References field.
- The page describes how AI tools were used.
- Every link opens publicly with no login, password or permission request.
- The project is submitted on the NASA site before Sunday 15 November, 23:59 local time.

## Part 2. Strategic recommendations

These are our own conclusions, not official guidance.

### What to prioritize

If Cottbus nominates at last year's global rate of about one in nine, a 19-team event sends about two teams forward. That is an estimate, not a published quota.

To be clearly in the top two on a five-part score, avoid a weak score anywhere. A project that scores 4 on all five beats one that scores 5 on two and 2 on the rest.

### Solve the brief exactly, then add one extension

Relevance explicitly scores "fully and directly addresses the challenge", so a loose reinterpretation costs points. Creativity is a separate score, and one sharp addition earns it.

For the fire calendar:

- **The brief:** a harmonized MODIS and VIIRS burning calendar in a web app.
- **The extension:** a live "abnormal now" alert that compares the current month with 25 years of history.

### Prototype, innovation and presentation

All five criteria count equally on paper. A working prototype is the main evidence for Validity and makes the Presentation credible, but it is not enough alone.

Split of remaining effort:

| Share | Work                                                    |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 60%   | Building the product                                    |
| 15%   | Evidence that it works: validation plot, measured error |
| 25%   | Project page, demo and pitch                            |

### Showing meaningful use of NASA data

Apply one test: if the NASA data were removed, would the product still exist? If yes, the data is decoration.

Then show the data doing work:

- Show the transformation, raw versus harmonized, side by side.
- Report a measured error on years the model did not train on.
- Present one finding only this data reveals, such as the 2012 sensor jump disappearing after harmonization.
- On the project page, name each dataset and say exactly how it is used.

### Demo and pitch

**The 30-second video.** Keep it at 29 seconds. The guide's suggested structure opens with the team, then the challenge at about 2 seconds, the project name and tagline at about 5 seconds, and closes from 20 to 30 seconds with the future the solution enables. Use the middle for the live product and the one result.

**The project page.** Judges score mostly from this page, so treat it as the main deliverable. Use headings that mirror the five criteria so a judge can score each one without searching.

**A live pitch.** Prepare a two-minute version in case the ending ceremony includes one. Open with the Brandenburg fire story, show the product, show the validation plot, end on the alert.

## Common mistakes

Rule-based. Each of these disqualifies the project or zeroes a score:

- A demo longer than 30 seconds or seven slides.
- A link that needs a login or permission.
- Non-English material.
- A teammate registered at a different event or missing from the Members tab.
- External code, text or images not listed in References.
- Missing either of the two deadlines.

Our own observations from the winners study:

- Calling a NASA API once and treating that as data use.
- Making a generic chatbot the differentiator.
- Leaving the project page to the last hour.
- Shipping a native app that judges cannot open.
- Adding features while the validation evidence is still missing.

## Open questions for the Local Lead

None of these are published. Ask today, because the answers change how we prepare.

1. How many teams will Cottbus nominate for global judging?
2. Do teams pitch live at the ending ceremony? How long, and with slides or a live product?
3. What exactly must be handed in by the 16:00 local deadline?
4. Can the NASA project page still be edited between 16:00 and 23:59?
5. Are there local prizes, and do the local challenges compete separately from the NASA ones?
